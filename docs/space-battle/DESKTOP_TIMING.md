# Why HUD Mean differs from headless

In this fresh Release run, tiering off produced 4.088 ms Mean at tick 30 and 0.758 ms for ticks 601–1200. Default runtime produced 5.797 ms at tick 10 and 0.667 ms for ticks 601–1200. High startup Mean was reproduced; it is not the steady warmed battle cost.

Five independent Release processes replay the same nominal game battle: seed 7331, 96 escorts and 3 carriers per team, LightSwarm, Tactical, reinforcements enabled. Core, SECS and adapter DLL hashes match; final checksums, counters and populations match.

Checksum: `4876EB52A0D4AFC3`. [Manifest](desktop-current/manifest.json).

Headless separately warms 600 ticks in another battle. Desktop records Step from cold startup, including initial JIT and lazy initialization. Default removes the DOTNET_TieredCompilation override; tiering0 explicitly disables tiered compilation. Paced desktop caps rendering at 60 frames/s, runs exactly one Step/frame and disables VSync: a controlled replay, not live accumulator FPS. Processes run sequentially.

HUD Mean averages the last min(600, completed tick count) samples. The first 10/30/60 ticks are short startup samples; after 600 it is a rolling window. Capture, rendering and frame waiting are outside Battle.LastTickMs. Their process load can affect the following Step.

This is one run per configuration; colour shows observed differences at matched tick ranges. A meaningful comparison needs the same battle settings, runtime, tick range, warmup and timer boundaries. Desktop Step timing starts at the first tick without warmup; headless runs a separate 600-tick warmup before measurement. These results do not establish the cause of a sustained value near 4 ms in another battle phase. RawCsvPath in the JSON contains only the diagnostic CSV filename; it does not reveal a local filesystem path. The [current high game benchmark on all five ECS](REPORT.md) contains 30 simulated minutes and opening windows. Its gameplay `ChurnMultiplier=2` differs from this desktop replay, so compare the timing scope and trajectory before interpreting the values.
